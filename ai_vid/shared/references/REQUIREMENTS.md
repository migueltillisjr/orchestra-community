# Requirements

## A. Single-Page Funnel Site

Create a single-page marketing and sales funnel site for **Info Pioneer**, a business process improvement and practical technology consulting company. The funnel should be designed to attract qualified small to mid-sized businesses, provide value before asking for a consultation, capture useful lead information, and guide the visitor toward the appropriate next step.

The funnel site must include:

1. A clear primary objective, such as encouraging visitors to complete the **Operational Friction Scorecard** or request a **Workflow Clarity Call**.
2. A strong hook that immediately addresses the visitor’s operational pain, such as messy workflows, repeated work, unclear handoffs, scattered documentation, or underused tools.
3. A clear explanation of the core problem Info Pioneer solves and why the problem matters to growing businesses.
4. A value-first lead magnet, such as the **Operational Friction Scorecard**, that helps visitors identify where their business is losing time, creating confusion, or relying too much on manual work.
5. A lead capture form that collects essential contact information and useful business context, including name, email, business name, business size, biggest operational issue, timeline, and a brief description of the current workflow problem.
6. A clear explanation of the next step after completing the scorecard, such as requesting a **Workflow Clarity Call**.
7. A paid offer section presenting the **Workflow Audit** as the first paid step.
8. A concise explanation of what the visitor receives from the Workflow Audit, including workflow assessment, friction mapping, process recommendations, documentation guidance, tool usage review, and an implementation path.
9. A clear fit section that identifies who the service is for and who it is not for.
10. Repeated calls to action that are clear, low-friction, and consistent throughout the page.

## B. Audience

Identify and define the target audience for the funnel site.

The audience should include small to mid-sized businesses, founder-led teams, consultancies, service businesses, operations teams, and growing organizations that already have steady revenue and are experiencing operational strain.

The funnel should be written for decision-makers or influential operators who are responsible for improving how work gets done inside the business. This may include founders, owners, operations managers, team leads, consultants, and administrative leaders.

The audience should influence the funnel’s communication style in the following ways:

1. Use practical, clear language instead of technical jargon.
2. Avoid overly salesy language.
3. Focus on pain points the audience already recognizes, such as repeated questions, manual handoffs, inconsistent tool use, and knowledge trapped with a few people.
4. Emphasize clarity, efficiency, privacy, and maintainability.
5. Present Info Pioneer as a helpful diagnostic partner rather than a pushy software or consulting vendor.
6. Make the next step feel useful and low-risk.

## C. Funnel Strategy

The funnel should follow a clear conversion path that moves the visitor from awareness to action.

The page should include the following funnel structure:

1. **Hook:** Capture attention by naming the hidden operational friction slowing the business down.
2. **Problem:** Explain how messy workflows, unclear handoffs, and scattered documentation create preventable drag.
3. **Belief Shift:** Reframe the issue by showing that the business may not need more software first; it may need clearer workflows and better internal systems.
4. **Front-End Value:** Offer the Operational Friction Scorecard as a free diagnostic tool.
5. **Lead Capture:** Collect contact information and business context.
6. **Diagnosis:** Let the visitor answer quick diagnostic questions and receive a friction score.
7. **Natural Next Step:** Guide qualified visitors toward a Workflow Clarity Call.
8. **Offer:** Present the Workflow Audit as the first paid engagement.
9. **Fit:** Clarify who is a good fit and who is not.
10. **Final CTA:** Encourage the visitor to take the scorecard or request a clarity call.

## D. Offer Requirements

The funnel must clearly present Info Pioneer’s offer without overwhelming the visitor.

The **Workflow Audit** should be positioned as the first paid step and should include:

1. Review of current workflows, tools, documentation, and team handoffs.
2. Identification of bottlenecks, repeated work, unclear ownership, and software waste.
3. Practical recommendations for the highest-impact improvements.
4. Process maps or workflow guidance for recurring work.
5. SOP or knowledge base recommendations.
6. Guidance for improving the value of existing tools before recommending new ones.
7. A prioritized action plan for implementation.

## E. Lead Capture and Qualification

The funnel must include a form or interactive section that captures useful lead information.

The form should collect:

1. Name
2. Email
3. Business name
4. Business size
5. Biggest operational issue
6. Timeline
7. Brief description of what feels repetitive, messy, or confusing

The funnel should qualify visitors based on:

1. Type of operational pain
2. Business size
3. Urgency or timeline
4. Readiness to act
5. Whether the visitor needs recommendations only or implementation support
6. Whether the business is a good fit for Info Pioneer’s services

## F. Professional Communication

The funnel must demonstrate professional communication in both content and presentation.

The site should:

1. Use polished, clear, and concise language.
2. Maintain a practical, trustworthy, and helpful tone.
3. Avoid exaggerated claims or unrealistic promises.
4. Avoid unnecessary jargon.
5. Use consistent terminology, including **Operational Friction Scorecard**, **Workflow Clarity Call**, and **Workflow Audit**.
6. Present Info Pioneer as a practical technology and process improvement partner.
7. Use strong visual hierarchy with clear headings, readable sections, and prominent calls to action.
8. Make the page easy to scan on both desktop and mobile.
9. Keep the visitor focused on one main conversion path.
10. Make the funnel feel helpful before it feels persuasive.

## G. Technical and Design Requirements

The final deliverable should be a single-page HTML funnel site.

The site should include:

1. Responsive design for desktop and mobile.
2. A hero section with a strong headline and primary call to action.
3. A lead capture form.
4. An interactive or clearly presented scorecard section.
5. A problem section.
6. A belief-shift or reframing section.
7. A service or offer section.
8. A fit section.
9. A final call-to-action section.
10. Clean, modern styling that supports trust and clarity.
11. Functional buttons and form behavior where practical.
12. No placeholder example sections, meta commentary, or instructional content intended only for the creator.
