# Rubric

This rubric evaluates a single-page marketing or sales funnel using value-first funnel best practices inspired by *DotCom Secrets*-style funnel design: clear hook, story, offer, lead capture, value ladder, qualification, follow-up, and conversion path.

| Section | Not Evident | Approaching Competence | Competent |
|---------|-------------|------------------------|-----------|
| **A. Funnel Objective** | The funnel does not have a clear business goal or next step. | The funnel has a general goal, but the desired user action is vague, competing, or hard to identify. | The funnel has one clear primary objective, such as capturing a lead, booking a call, starting an application, or selling a front-end offer. |
| **A1. Target Audience** | The funnel does not identify who it is for. | The funnel names a broad audience, but the message is not specific to their pain, readiness, or buying context. | The funnel clearly identifies a specific audience and speaks directly to their current pain, desired outcome, and level of awareness. |
| **A2. Core Problem** | The funnel does not clearly define the problem being solved. | The funnel mentions a problem, but it is too broad, generic, or disconnected from the offer. | The funnel clearly explains the painful problem, why it matters, and what happens if it remains unsolved. |
| **B. Hook** | The page lacks a strong opening hook. | The hook is present but generic, unclear, or focused too much on the company instead of the prospect. | The hook immediately grabs attention by naming a specific pain, desire, misconception, or hidden problem the prospect cares about. |
| **B1. Big Idea** | The funnel does not present a compelling central idea. | The funnel has a central message, but it is not memorable or differentiated. | The funnel presents a clear big idea that reframes the prospect’s problem and makes the solution feel timely, useful, and different. |
| **B2. Curiosity and Relevance** | The funnel gives the visitor no reason to continue reading. | The funnel creates some interest, but the copy does not build enough curiosity or urgency. | The funnel creates curiosity while staying relevant to the prospect’s real problem, making them want to keep moving through the page. |
| **C. Story** | The funnel does not include a story, narrative, or problem journey. | The funnel includes some background, but it does not connect emotionally or logically to the offer. | The funnel uses story or narrative to explain why the problem exists, why common solutions fail, and why this approach makes sense. |
| **C1. Belief Shift** | The funnel does not challenge old assumptions or create a new way of thinking. | The funnel hints at a different perspective, but the belief shift is weak or unclear. | The funnel clearly moves the prospect from an old belief to a new belief that supports the offer. |
| **C2. Emotional Connection** | The funnel feels purely informational and does not connect with the visitor’s frustration or desire. | The funnel mentions emotions or pain points but does not make them vivid or specific. | The funnel reflects the visitor’s real frustrations, fears, goals, and desired relief in a way that feels accurate and human. |
| **D. Offer** | The funnel does not present a clear offer. | The offer is present, but the value, deliverables, or outcome are unclear. | The offer is specific, easy to understand, outcome-oriented, and clearly connected to the problem introduced earlier. |
| **D1. Offer Stack** | The funnel does not explain what the prospect receives. | The funnel lists some deliverables, but they are incomplete, vague, or not tied to outcomes. | The funnel clearly stacks the offer with deliverables, benefits, bonuses, support, timelines, or other value elements. |
| **D2. Risk Reduction** | The funnel does not reduce perceived risk. | The funnel includes a weak reassurance, but it does not address the prospect’s likely concerns. | The funnel reduces risk by clarifying expectations, scope, timeline, next steps, privacy, support, or fit. |
| **D3. Call to Action** | The funnel has no clear call to action. | The funnel has a call to action, but it is vague, hidden, repeated inconsistently, or asks for too much too soon. | The funnel uses clear, repeated, low-friction calls to action that match the visitor’s stage of awareness. |
| **E. Value Ladder** | The funnel does not show a path from free value to paid engagement. | The funnel includes multiple offers, but the progression between them is unclear. | The funnel presents a logical value ladder, such as free resource, consultation, paid audit, implementation, and ongoing support. |
| **E1. Front-End Value** | The funnel does not offer value before asking for a sale or meeting. | The funnel offers limited value, but it feels thin, generic, or disconnected from the paid offer. | The funnel gives useful front-end value, such as a checklist, scorecard, guide, calculator, mini-audit, template, or diagnostic. |
| **E2. Natural Next Step** | The free offer does not lead logically to the paid offer. | The connection between the free offer and the paid offer is present but weak. | The free offer reveals a problem or opportunity that naturally leads to the next step, such as a call, audit, or implementation project. |
| **F. Lead Capture** | The funnel does not collect contact information. | The funnel collects basic contact information but does not gather enough context to qualify or personalize follow-up. | The funnel captures contact information and useful context, such as pain point, business size, tools used, urgency, timeline, and type of help needed. |
| **F1. Form Friction** | The form is too long, confusing, or intimidating for the value being offered. | The form is usable but may ask for too much too early or not enough to qualify the lead. | The form balances conversion and qualification by collecting essential information first and optional deeper context second. |
| **F2. Data Usefulness** | The captured data would not help with segmentation, qualification, or follow-up. | Some useful data is captured, but it is not clearly connected to the sales process. | The captured data directly supports segmentation, lead scoring, follow-up messaging, and offer recommendation. |
| **G. Qualification** | The funnel treats every visitor as equally qualified. | The funnel asks some qualification questions, but they are incomplete or not tied to the offer. | The funnel qualifies prospects based on pain, urgency, budget readiness, business fit, decision role, and implementation need. |
| **G1. Application Framing** | The funnel makes the next step feel like a generic sales call. | The funnel avoids the phrase “sales call” but does not clearly frame the call as useful or selective. | The funnel frames the next step as a useful diagnostic, clarity call, consultation, or application process that protects both sides from poor fit. |
| **G2. Segmentation by Pain** | The funnel does not segment leads by problem type. | The funnel identifies different pain points but does not connect them to different messages or offers. | The funnel segments leads by pain type and routes them toward the most relevant message, resource, offer, or follow-up path. |
| **H. Follow-Up Sequence** | The funnel has no follow-up plan after lead capture. | The funnel has a follow-up plan, but it is mostly promotional or too short. | The funnel includes a nurture sequence that educates, builds trust, addresses objections, and guides the prospect toward the next step. |
| **H1. Email Content Strategy** | Follow-up emails are not planned or do not provide value. | Follow-up emails provide some value, but they are repetitive, generic, or disconnected from the funnel promise. | Follow-up emails deliver the resource, explain the problem, provide quick wins, share examples, address objections, and invite the next step. |
| **H2. Objection Handling** | The funnel does not address objections. | The funnel addresses some objections but misses major concerns such as cost, complexity, time, trust, or fit. | The funnel directly addresses likely objections and reframes them in a way that supports informed action. |
| **I. Page Structure** | The page is disorganized or hard to follow. | The page has a basic structure, but the sections do not create a strong conversion path. | The page follows a clear conversion sequence: hook, problem, story, value, offer, proof, qualification, CTA, and follow-up path. |
| **I1. Visual Hierarchy** | The design does not guide attention to the most important message or action. | The design is readable but lacks emphasis, contrast, or clear section priority. | The design uses headings, spacing, contrast, cards, buttons, and section flow to guide the visitor toward the primary action. |
| **I2. Message Consistency** | The page uses inconsistent language, offers, or calls to action. | The message is mostly consistent but includes some confusing or competing terms. | The page uses consistent language for the problem, offer, lead magnet, call, and paid next step. |
| **J. Trust and Credibility** | The funnel provides no reason to trust the business. | The funnel includes some credibility signals, but they are generic or unsupported. | The funnel includes credibility signals such as examples, use cases, process clarity, founder expertise, client outcomes, testimonials, or before-and-after improvements. |
| **J1. Proof Elements** | The funnel includes no proof, examples, or demonstrations. | The funnel includes proof, but it is weak, vague, or not directly related to the offer. | The funnel uses relevant proof such as case examples, sample outputs, screenshots, metrics, testimonials, or pilot results. |
| **J2. Specificity** | The funnel relies on broad claims and generic language. | The funnel includes some specifics, but many claims remain vague. | The funnel uses specific pains, deliverables, timelines, examples, outcomes, and next steps. |
| **K. Offer Fit and Ethics** | The funnel pressures visitors into an offer regardless of fit. | The funnel mentions fit but does not clearly define who should or should not proceed. | The funnel clearly states who the offer is for, who it is not for, and what conditions make the engagement worthwhile. |
| **K1. Non-Salesy Positioning** | The funnel feels pushy, manipulative, or overly sales-driven. | The funnel has some value-first language but still feels overly promotional. | The funnel feels helpful, diagnostic, and practical while still guiding qualified prospects toward action. |
| **K2. Practicality of Promise** | The funnel makes unrealistic or exaggerated claims. | The funnel makes mostly realistic claims but could overstate speed, certainty, or outcomes. | The funnel makes practical, believable promises that match the service scope and evidence provided. |
| **L. Conversion Measurement** | The funnel does not define what should be measured. | The funnel includes some metrics, but they are incomplete or not tied to funnel performance. | The funnel defines key metrics such as visitors, opt-ins, scorecard completions, call bookings, show-up rate, close rate, source quality, and common pain points. |
| **L1. Optimization Plan** | The funnel does not include a plan to improve based on results. | The funnel includes general improvement ideas but no clear testing approach. | The funnel includes a plan to test and improve headlines, lead magnets, form fields, CTAs, email sequence, offer framing, and qualification questions. |
| **M. Overall Funnel Quality** | The funnel is incomplete, unclear, or unlikely to convert qualified visitors. | The funnel has several useful parts, but the strategy, messaging, or conversion path needs refinement. | The funnel presents a strong, value-first conversion path that attracts the right audience, captures useful data, builds trust, qualifies leads, and guides them toward the right paid next step. |

## Recommended Scoring Guide

| Rating | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **0 - Not Evident** | The section is missing or does not meet the requirement. |
| **1 - Approaching Competence** | The section is present but incomplete, unclear, generic, or weakly connected to the funnel objective. |
| **2 - Competent** | The section is clear, specific, aligned with the funnel objective, and useful for conversion. |

## Competency Thresholds

| Score Range | Interpretation |
|-------------|----------------|
| **0–29** | Funnel is underdeveloped and needs major strategy, offer, and structure improvements. |
| **30–49** | Funnel has a workable foundation but needs stronger positioning, offer clarity, lead capture, and follow-up. |
| **50–64** | Funnel is solid and likely usable, but should be improved with stronger proof, qualification, testing, and objection handling. |
| **65–74** | Funnel is strong, value-first, conversion-focused, and ready for launch or active testing. |
