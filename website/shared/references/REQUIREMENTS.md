# P.R.A.Y. Website Requirements

## 1. Product Definition

Build a modern, responsive, trustworthy informational website for **P.R.A.Y.**, short for **Positive Reinforcement for America’s Youth**. The site should help families, young people, community members, volunteers, donors, and potential partners understand the organization’s purpose, values, history, and work.

The website must feel hopeful, safe, inclusive, community-centered, and action-oriented. It must not feel like a generic charity template, an entertainment site, or a commercial sales funnel.

### Confirmed source information

| Topic | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Organization name | P.R.A.Y. / Positive Reinforcement for America’s Youth |
| Founder | Keith Smith |
| Primary audience | Underprivileged youth, families, supporters, volunteers, and community partners |
| Geographic focus | The Fox Valley and surrounding areas |
| Long-term goal | Provide a safe place for youth to connect outside school and away from the streets, and host family events, especially for the recovery community. |
| Mission | Empower underprivileged youth through opportunities, safe spaces, positive role models, teamwork, service, love, patience, and understanding. |
| Vision | Create positive experiences, healthy social environments, community connections, and opportunities that support the spiritual and emotional growth of youth. |
| Existing social channels | Instagram: `https://www.instagram.com/praygram4america`; YouTube: `https://youtube.com/@pray4americasyouth?si=Rqiqpzs0jRWfsR3k` |

Do not expand these claims with statistics, program outcomes, partner names, testimonials, donation promises, or legal designations until the organization supplies and approves them.

## 2. Primary Website Outcomes

The first visit should enable a person to:

1. Understand what P.R.A.Y. is and whom it serves within five seconds.
2. Understand the organization’s mission, vision, history, and the kind of safe, positive experiences it creates.
3. Learn about P.R.A.Y.’s programs, community focus, founder, and service area.
4. Trust that the website represents a real community effort with transparent information and respectful treatment of young people.
5. Access the site comfortably on a phone, with keyboard navigation, readable text, and no dependence on motion or hover.

## 3. Information Architecture

Use a simple, shallow navigation structure. The final labels may be refined with the organization, but the website should support these content areas:

| Page or section | Purpose | Required content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home | Establish purpose and direct visitors to the most relevant information. | P.R.A.Y. name, expanded name, concise description, supporting imagery, mission or impact preview, and social links. |
| About | Build understanding and trust. | Mission, vision, founder introduction for Keith Smith, geographic focus, values, and the long-term goal. |
| Programs or What We Do | Explain how P.R.A.Y. serves youth. | Confirmed programs, activities, family events, recovery-community work, eligibility or participation details, and program status. Do not invent program names. |
| Resources or Events | Provide useful, current information for visitors to read. | Approved events, youth or family resources, announcements, and clear dates. Include an honest empty state when nothing is published. Do not include registration or RSVP functionality. |
| Contact or Organization Details | Provide verified reference information. | Approved email, phone, location or service area, social links, and safeguarding information where appropriate. Do not include a contact form or submission workflow. |

If the initial release is a single page, represent these areas as clearly labeled sections with anchored navigation and clear links to related informational sections.

## 4. Homepage Requirements

### Hero

- Display **P.R.A.Y.** and **Positive Reinforcement for America’s Youth** prominently.
- Communicate the organization’s role in one short, plain-language statement.
- Name the service area only when the Fox Valley scope is still current and approved for public display.
- Include clear informational links, such as `Learn about our work`, `Read our mission`, or `Meet the founder`.
- Do not include donation, volunteer, registration, application, prayer-submission, or other transactional calls to action.
- Use authentic, approved imagery or a deliberate typographic composition. Do not use unrelated stock photos of children or imply participation by people who are not affiliated with P.R.A.Y.

### Mission and vision

- Present the mission and vision as separate, scannable blocks.
- Preserve the meaning of the source statements while editing for web readability.
- Avoid burying the organization’s purpose under decorative copy or unexplained acronyms.

### Community and impact

- Explain the safe-space goal, positive role modeling, teamwork, service, family events, and recovery-community focus.
- Show how a visitor can support or participate without claiming outcomes that have not been measured.
- Prefer real stories, approved quotations, event documentation, or transparent program descriptions over invented impact statistics.

### Trust and next steps

- Introduce the founder with an approved portrait and biography, or omit the portrait rather than using a placeholder.
- Link to the verified Instagram and YouTube accounts with accessible labels.
- Make verified organization details and social links easy to find from the homepage and footer.
- Include a concise footer with organization name, approved contact information, social links, copyright, and required legal or privacy links.

## 5. Content and Editorial Requirements

- Use warm, direct, plain-language writing appropriate for families and community supporters.
- Define `P.R.A.Y.` on first use in the main content.
- Use “youth,” “young people,” “families,” and other audience terms consistently after confirming the organization’s preferred language.
- Avoid deficit-focused, sensational, or stigmatizing descriptions of young people, poverty, recovery, or neighborhood conditions.
- Do not publish a child’s name, face, story, prayer request, or personal information without documented permission and an approved publishing process.
- Every event, program, testimonial, statistic, address, phone number, and email must have an owner and review date.
- Use informational links only. Do not add donation, volunteer, registration, application, prayer-submission, or other transactional calls to action.
- Add visible last-updated dates for time-sensitive events, programs, and announcements.
- Provide useful empty states instead of placeholder text such as “Coming soon” when a section has no approved content.

## 6. Visual and Interaction Direction

- Create a recognizable visual identity from the P.R.A.Y. name, mission, and community focus rather than copying the current site’s sparse builder layout.
- Use an optimistic, human palette with strong text contrast and enough neutral space for calm reading.
- Use expressive but highly readable typography. Keep headings distinctive and body text comfortable on phones.
- Use real, approved community imagery as evidence of the work. Never use dark, anonymous, exploitative, or misleading imagery to manufacture emotion.
- Establish a clear hierarchy: organization identity, purpose, proof or explanation, then action.
- Use restrained motion for page entry or section reveals only when it clarifies structure. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Use familiar icons for social, navigation, and actions, with accessible names and tooltips when an icon is not self-explanatory.
- Keep repeated items consistent, but do not make every section a floating card. Use full-width bands and unframed content areas where appropriate.
- Avoid oversized marketing claims, dense visual effects, autoplay media, and decorative elements that compete with the mission.

## 7. Responsive Requirements

- Support viewport widths of at least 320px, 375px, 768px, 1024px, and 1440px.
- Do not allow horizontal scrolling, clipped text, overlapping content, or controls that move when labels wrap.
- Make navigation usable by touch and keyboard. If a mobile menu is used, it must have a clear open state, close control, focus management, and Escape-key support.
- Keep primary actions visible and large enough to activate comfortably on touch devices.
- Preserve image subjects and readable focal points at every breakpoint.
- Ensure long mission statements, event titles, social links, and form errors wrap inside their containers.
- Support increased text size and browser zoom without hiding essential content or causing interaction failure.

## 8. Accessibility Requirements

- Use semantic landmarks, one clear page `h1`, logical heading order, and descriptive link text.
- Provide meaningful alt text for informative images and empty alt text for decorative images.
- Ensure keyboard access to every interactive element with a visible `:focus-visible` treatment.
- Meet WCAG 2.2 AA contrast expectations for text, controls, focus indicators, and meaningful graphics.
- Label every form field, identify required fields, provide useful validation, and announce errors to assistive technology.
- Never communicate status, category, or required action by color alone.
- Provide captions or transcripts for video and text alternatives for meaningful audio or visual content.
- Respect reduced motion, forced colors, increased text spacing, and touch input.
- Do not expose private youth information or collect more personal information than the approved workflow requires.

## 9. Functional Requirements

| Feature | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Navigation | All navigation links resolve to real sections or pages. Current location is understandable without relying on color alone. |
| Social links | Instagram and YouTube links open the verified accounts and have descriptive accessible names. |
| Organization details | Approved contact and service-area information is displayed as reference information only. Do not include a contact form or submission workflow. |
| Events | Dates, times, locations, and cancellation or update information are explicit when events are described. Do not include registration or RSVP functionality. |
| Transactional features | Donations, volunteering, applications, partner inquiries, prayer requests, event registration, and other submissions are out of scope. Do not simulate these flows. |
| Media | Video embeds, images, and social content have responsive dimensions, fallbacks, captions or labels where needed, and no autoplay with sound. |
| Errors | Missing pages, failed forms, empty event lists, and unavailable media have helpful, branded states with a path forward. |

## 10. Technical Requirements

- Prefer a lightweight static implementation unless the approved requirements demand a CMS or application backend.
- Keep source HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and assets organized and maintainable. Do not make generated output the only editable source unless explicitly documented.
- Use local assets where available and provide stable relative paths for the final output.
- Compress and size images for their rendered dimensions; reserve modern formats for browsers that support them and provide fallbacks when necessary.
- Avoid unnecessary third-party scripts, trackers, font requests, and embeds. Document every external dependency.
- Set a meaningful document title, meta description, canonical URL when available, Open Graph metadata, language, favicon, and social preview image.
- Ensure direct loading of a deep link works when the site uses more than one route.
- Keep the browser console free of avoidable errors and warnings.
- Do not place secrets or private API keys in client-side code or committed files.
- Provide a reproducible build or clearly identify the maintained final HTML output.

## 11. Acceptance Criteria

The website is ready for review when:

- A first-time visitor can identify P.R.A.Y., its audience, its service area, and its purpose within five seconds.
- The mission, vision, founder, long-term goal, and verified social links are present or explicitly marked as awaiting approved content.
- Every visible link leads to a real informational section, page, or verified social account and does not create a dead end.
- The site works at the required mobile and desktop widths without horizontal overflow or visual overlap.
- Keyboard navigation, focus states, headings, labels, alt text, contrast, and reduced-motion behavior pass manual review.
- Images and embedded media are approved, responsive, performant, and respectful of youth privacy.
- No fabricated facts, placeholder content, broken links, exposed secrets, or avoidable console errors remain.
- The output path, build method, content gaps, and verification results are documented for the next reviewer.

## 12. Open Decisions Before Launch

Confirm these with P.R.A.Y. before implementing related sections:

- Legal organization name, nonprofit status, and required footer disclosures.
- Approved logo, brand colors, typography, and image library.
- Final service area wording and whether locations beyond the Fox Valley should be named.
- Current programs, program owners, schedules, age ranges, eligibility, and safeguarding practices.
- Public contact email, phone number, mailing address, and response expectations.
- Whether a future phase should add donations, volunteering, event registration, prayer requests, or partner inquiries. These are out of scope for this informational release.
- Approved analytics, video, and social embed providers, if any are needed.
- Photography permissions, youth safeguarding policy, privacy policy, cookie requirements, and content review process.
- Launch domain, hosting, redirect behavior, analytics ownership, and ongoing maintenance responsibility.
